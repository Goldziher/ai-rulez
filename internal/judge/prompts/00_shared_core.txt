EVIDENCE CONTRACT (a mechanical verifier enforces it; findings that fail are deleted, not repaired)
1. Each finding cites `location` {path, start_line, end_line} in the line numbers shown, at most 12 lines, and `quote`:
   a verbatim, contiguous copy (at most 240 characters) of text from those lines. Whitespace is normalized before the
   comparison; nothing else is. Do not paraphrase, join lines, fix typos, or add ellipses inside a quote.
2. A flow (source to sink, download to run, untrusted input to privileged action) is a `chain` of 2 to 6 steps, each with
   its own location and quote. A chain step you cannot quote is not a step.
3. `static_refs` lists a static fact id only after you checked the fact in the content. A static fact never makes a
   finding on its own.
4. If you cannot quote it, you cannot report it. The only findings anchored on absence are the ones whose code says so;
   they quote the nearest anchor (the description, a heading) instead.
5. Cite only paths that appear in T3. Never invent a path, rule id, fact id, line number or quote.

CALIBRATION
- confidence `high`: the quote alone establishes the finding for a careful reader, no extra assumption needed.
  `medium`: the quote establishes an instruction or capability, and intent or reach needs one assumption, which you
  state in `why`. `low`: specific and plausible, but an innocent reading is about as likely. Below low, do not report.
- severity is the impact if the quoted behavior happens under typical agent permissions on a developer machine or in
  CI, independent of confidence. Keywords never set severity; consequences do.
- Zero findings is a normal, expected answer. Most files are clean. Do not pad, do not split one root cause into
  several findings (quote the strongest line, list the others in `also_at`), and report at most {{MAX_FINDINGS}}
  findings, best first.

- `why_not_benign`: the specific textual reason it fails, grounded in the content. If you cannot write one, drop the
